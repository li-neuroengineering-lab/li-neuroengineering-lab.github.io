PhD opportunity · Fall 2027

Experimental Neuroscience & Epilepsy

One opening in the Biomedical Engineering PhD program for a researcher ready to develop as an independent experimental neuroscientist.

Research & training

Our NIH-supported research investigates how pathological brain networks develop during epileptogenesis, integrating rodent models, electrophysiology, and brain imaging. Doctoral training includes experimental design, troubleshooting, data interpretation, and scientific writing.

Who should apply

Applicants should have sustained hands-on rodent research experience beyond coursework. A research-based master’s degree or substantial research assistant or technician experience is strongly preferred. Experience with rodent surgery, in vivo electrophysiology, or brain histology is particularly valuable; advanced programming is not required.

Project details, funding & application

Experimental work

  • Electrode fabrication, stereotaxic surgery, and chronic electrode implantation in rodents.
  • Longitudinal EEG/LFP acquisition, recording quality checks, and behavioral testing.
  • Perfusion, brain tissue collection, sectioning, staining, and histology or immunohistochemistry.

Students receive close mentorship from Dr. Li and experienced lab members. Careful records, reproducible procedures, and responsible animal care are central to the work.

Background & funding

Relevant backgrounds include neuroscience, biology, physiology, pharmacology, biomedical engineering, and related fields. Applicants are not expected to have every listed skill. We value reliability, initiative, attention to detail, clear communication, and the ability to learn from experimental setbacks.

Research assistantship support is available through NIH-funded projects. Stipend, tuition, benefits, and support and renewal conditions will be specified in the formal offer. Research is based at UNT in Denton, within the Dallas–Fort Worth metropolitan area.

How to apply

Email Dr. Lin Li with your CV and a brief statement of research interests and experimental experience. Describe the procedures you personally performed, approximate animal or sample numbers, duration of experience, level of independence, and one example of troubleshooting. You may include relevant publications or other research outputs, indicating your contribution.

Inquiries are reviewed on a rolling basis. Applicants are encouraged to complete the formal UNT application by January 15, 2027 for consideration across funding opportunities. Admission requires meeting the BME PhD program criteria, including applicable testing requirements. Qualified applicants of all backgrounds are welcome.

Undergraduate / TAMS opportunities

Undergraduate Research Training

Join a faculty-mentored team and gain hands-on experience in neuroengineering and experimental neuroscience.

Research & training

Work alongside graduate students and the PI on epilepsy research, photobiomodulation, electrophysiology and neural recording, histology and immunohistochemistry, literature review, and research documentation.

Who should apply

We welcome motivated undergraduate and TAMS students with a strong interest in neuroscience or biomedical research. Previous research experience is not required; training is provided. Applicants should be responsible, organized, willing to learn, and able to commit 8–10 hours per week for at least two semesters.

Training benefits & application

Develop laboratory, scientific communication, and data-analysis skills, with opportunities for posters, abstracts, and possible publications. MATLAB or Python experience is a plus, but not required.

How to apply

Email Dr. Lin Li your CV or résumé and a brief introduction including your major, year, research interests, and availability. See the recruitment flyer for more information.
